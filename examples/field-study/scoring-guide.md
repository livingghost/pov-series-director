# Direct Observation Guide

This guide keeps evaluation tied to visible or audible facts. It is not a universal quality score and it does not collapse unrelated outcomes into one number.

## Binary observations

Use yes/no only for a clearly defined event:

- a cut occurs;
- the guardian reaches a seated state;
- the same prop remains in both hands;
- the mattress visibly responds at contact;
- speech occurs;
- the exact requested words are audible;
- the visible speaker matches the intended speaker;
- an extra person appears.

## Start/source comparison

Compare concrete features:

- camera direction, height, roll, and framing;
- subject count, screen position, scale, and pose;
- hand/prop relationship;
- major furniture and path geometry;
- light-source direction and exposure relationship;
- clothing colors and defining marks;
- source-video timing/camera/contact when the operation should preserve them.

Record each difference rather than writing “high fidelity.”

## Sequence and causality

Write requested and observed order:

```text
requested: thunder → jolt → ears turn → head follows → step → sit → mattress compresses → cloth settles → gaze returns
observed: jolt → immediate cut → already seated → gaze returns
```

Then identify the first divergence. This gives a more useful revision target than “motion adherence: poor.”

## Contact and material response

Record separately:

- moving limb/body owner;
- contact point and side;
- occlusion order;
- whether weight/support is plausible;
- mattress, cloth, fur, water, snow, or object response;
- final body and prop state.

## Performance

Describe observable change:

- gaze order;
- shoulder/posture change;
- hand tension or release;
- anatomy-specific reaction;
- breath/idle behavior;
- whether the intended emotional transition can be read without the production notes.

Do not score an emotion label when the visible behavior is ambiguous.

## Dialogue and audio

Record separately:

- speech present;
- apparent speaker;
- exact transcript heard;
- visible lip movement and synchronization;
- timing window;
- voice continuity;
- rain/ambience/effect continuity;
- masking or mix problems.

## Endpoint usefulness

Record:

- camera state;
- character pose/facing/gaze;
- hands and prop ownership;
- contact state;
- light/weather state;
- whether the next planned first verb is physically possible;
- whether repair/composite is required before continuation.

## Repeatability statement

Useful:

```text
Three of four variants completed the sit. Two of four kept the shirt in both hands. One of four showed visible mattress compression. One of four introduced a cut.
```

Not useful:

```text
motion adherence: good
```

## Human decision

A variant may be accepted when a flexible detail differs. Preserve the observation and record the user's waiver; do not rewrite the result to match the plan.
